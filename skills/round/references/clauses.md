# Term sheet clauses

Plain-English explanations for `/round term-sheet`. "Standard" below means **what the widely used
model documents default to** — NVCA model term sheet (US), BVCA model documents (UK), Series Seed,
Y Combinator's post-money SAFE — not a measured market rate. When the founder needs a market rate
(how common a term is this quarter), it comes from a named, dated source or from their lawyer.

## Economics

| Clause | What it is | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Price / valuation** | Pre-money (before the money) + investment = post-money. Investor ownership = investment ÷ post-money | Is the valuation stated pre or post? Is it "fully diluted" — and what does that include (pool, SAFEs, notes, warrants)? |
| **Option pool shuffle** | A new or enlarged employee pool counted in the pre-money, so its dilution falls on existing holders, not the new investor | Size of the pool and its basis: is it sized to a hiring plan for the period the round funds? Effective pre-money = pre-money − pool top-up value. Ask whether the pool can be sized to the plan or partly post-money |
| **Liquidation preference** | On a sale or wind-down, preferred holders take their money back before common | Multiple (model documents: 1x), seniority (pari passu vs stacked senior), and what counts as a liquidation event |
| **Participation** | Participating preferred takes its preference **and** its share of what is left; non-participating chooses the greater of the two | Model documents default to non-participating. Participation, especially uncapped, is a question for counsel |
| **Dividends** | Usually non-cumulative, payable only if declared | Cumulative dividends accrue and add to the preference — flag |
| **Anti-dilution** | Protects investors if a later round is at a lower price | Broad-based weighted average is the model default. Narrow-based is heavier; **full ratchet** is rare and harsh — flag. Check carve-outs (pool grants, conversions) |
| **Conversion** | Preferred converts to common, voluntarily or automatically (e.g. at an IPO) | Automatic-conversion thresholds set very high give investors a block on an IPO |
| **Pro rata / pre-emption** | Right to keep their percentage in future rounds | Normal for leads. Super pro rata (more than their share) can crowd out the next lead — flag |
| **Redemption** | Investors can force the company to buy back shares after a period | Uncommon in model documents; a question for counsel |

## Control

| Clause | What it is | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Board** | Who sits on the board and who appoints them | Founder/common control at seed is typical; count seats after this round and the next. Independent seat: who chooses them? |
| **Protective provisions** | Matters needing the preferred holders' (or the lead's) consent | Model lists cover changes to the preferred's rights, new senior shares, a sale, dissolution. Vetoes over ordinary operations (budgets, hiring, contracts) are a flag. Who votes: a majority of all preferred, or one investor alone? |
| **Information rights** | What the company must report, how often | Monthly management accounts and annual audited accounts for major investors are common; audit costs at seed, or rights for every small holder, are worth asking about |
| **Founder vesting** | Founders' shares vest over time; unvested shares can be bought back if they leave | Re-vesting already-held shares, credit for time served, acceleration on a sale (single vs double trigger), good-/bad-leaver definitions (UK) |
| **Drag-along** | A majority can force everyone to sell in an approved sale | Thresholds: who must approve (board, majority of common, majority of preferred); minimum price protections |
| **Tag-along / co-sale** | If founders sell, investors may sell alongside | Normal; check it doesn't block small secondary sales |
| **Right of first refusal (ROFR)** | Company/investors may buy shares a holder wants to sell | Normal; check scope |

## Process

| Clause | What it is | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **No-shop / exclusivity** | The company stops talking to other investors for a period | Length (model documents bracket it for negotiation), whether it ends if the investor walks or slows, whether it covers the rest of this round's investors |
| **Binding vs non-binding** | Most terms are non-binding; confidentiality, no-shop, costs and governing law usually bind | Anything else stated as binding — flag |
| **Legal fees** | The company often pays the lead's legal costs | Is there a cap? |
| **Conditions to closing** | Diligence, documents, approvals | Open-ended conditions keep the investor's option open — flag |
| **Expiry** | When the offer lapses | Very short expiry is pressure; note it, don't act on it |

## SAFEs and convertible notes

If the document is a SAFE or note, the clauses are: valuation cap (pre- or post-money), discount, most
favoured nation, pro rata side letter, maturity and interest (notes only), and conversion mechanics.
A **post-money** SAFE cap fixes the holder's percentage before the priced round — so stacking several
dilutes the founders more than it looks; model the stack with the cap table.
