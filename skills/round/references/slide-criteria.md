# Slide criteria

What each section of a fundraising deck must do, and how the bar moves with stage. Order is a
default, not a rule: lead with the strongest section (often traction at seed and Series A, team at
pre-seed), but the reader must never wait past slide five to learn what the company does and why it
might win.

## 1. Sections

| Section | It must | Common failures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Title / one-liner** | Say what the company does in plain words, for whom | A tagline instead of a description; buzzwords |
| **Problem** | A specific customer, a specific pain, evidence it is painful (cost, time, frequency, quotes) | Problem too broad; no evidence; describing a solution's absence |
| **Solution / product** | How it solves that pain, shown (screenshot, demo flow), why it is better than today's workaround | Feature lists; no visuals; claims without proof |
| **Why now** | What changed (technology, regulation, cost curve, behaviour) that makes this possible or urgent now | Missing entirely; generic "AI is big" |
| **Market** | Bottom-up sizing (customers × price), the beachhead, how it expands; sources stated | Top-down "1% of a $X bn market"; unsourced; inconsistent with business model |
| **Traction** | The one metric that matters for this business, over time, with definitions; logos, pilots, retention | Cumulative charts hiding flat growth; vanity metrics; undefined terms |
| **Business model** | Who pays, how much, how it scales; unit economics as far as the stage allows | Pricing missing; margins hand-waved |
| **Go-to-market** | How customers are acquired, what has worked so far, the repeatable channel | "Viral", "partnerships" with no evidence |
| **Competition** | Honest landscape including the status quo; the real differentiation and why it lasts | "No competitors"; a 2×2 drawn to win; missing obvious incumbents |
| **Team** | Why this team wins this market: relevant experience, unfair insight, key hires to come | Logos without roles; missing who does what |
| **Ask and use of funds** | Amount, instrument if set, what it buys (milestones, runway), how it's split | No milestones; use of funds by department only; ask inconsistent with the plan |

## 2. Stage lens

- **Pre-seed**: team and insight carry most of the weight; problem evidence (customer interviews,
  waitlist, LOIs) stands in for traction. Product can be early; the reason this team can build it
  cannot be vague.
- **Seed**: evidence it works — early revenue, usage, retention or pilots converting; a believable
  first go-to-market motion; the milestones this round buys toward a Series A.
- **Series A**: a repeatable engine — growth over consecutive periods, retention or net revenue
  retention, unit economics trending right, a channel that scales, the team to scale it. Metrics
  must be precise and defined; a data pack will be asked for.

## 3. The whole deck

- Headlines as claims. Reading only the headlines should tell the story.
- One idea per slide; the send-ahead version stands alone (no "as I'll explain").
- Numbers agree everywhere: market, ask, runway, the pipeline's target.
- 10–15 slides for the main deck; detail goes in an appendix.
- Contact details and a date on the last slide.
